# Model releases

> New model launches, capabilities, versions, deprecations, API changes.

_27 active · 0 stale · synthesis updated 2026-11-23_

## Current best understanding

**Model tiering is now a design decision (Nov 2026).** Anthropic's lineup runs Haiku 5.5 (fastest and cheapest, for routing, classification and summarization), Sonnet 5.5 (half Opus price, for well-scoped tasks), Opus 5.5 (a 'cheap Fable' daily driver) and Fable 5.2 (2x Opus, for loosely defined multi-milestone goals with explicit non-negotiables). Google ships Gemini 4 Hydrogen (fast, ~60% Terminal Bench 4), Gemini 4 Carbon (tops Cursor, Zapier and Perplexity benches at 2–9x lower cost) and Gemini 4 Argon (long-horizon work via Teamwork Preview and /boost subagents). OpenAI has GPT-6.1 Sol and GPT-6 Astra, plus the Ultrafast mode (8x faster at 6x cost), which is best for hot-reload UI iteration. 'Fable class' models solve well-stated goals over ~12-hour runs, so the human work becomes defining the goal and provisioning tools. For agentic UX, that means surfacing goals, constraints and cost-per-task. Assigning the cheapest capable model to each sub-agent (e.g. DeepSeek V4 Flash) is a credible pattern.

**New interaction surfaces.** GPT-Live 1 is full-duplex voice that handles turn-taking itself, with no separate VAD model, which changes how voice agent UI handles interruption and state. In the browser, LiteRT-LM.js runs Gemma 4 in under 20 lines over WebGPU/Wasm, and LiteRT.js runs embedding models. That makes local, framework-agnostic AI components realistic. Gemma 4 comes in 2B and 4B (audio-capable) and 12B, 26B MoE and 31B (text and image). Dynamic quantization keeps quality high: GLM 5.3 at 4-bit retains ~94%. On API change management, Anthropic treats the model string as an implicit version and softens deprecations with toggles and beta headers, as in the move from extended to adaptive thinking.

**Gated cyber models and eval skepticism.** Claude Mythos shares weights with Fable but sits behind the KYC Cyber Verification Program (Project Glasswing). OpenAI's GPT-6 Cyber reports 52% on ExploitGym vs ~38% for Mythos 5.2 and stays in scope 99.9–100% of evaluated cases. Public benchmarks such as the pelican SVG test get trained against quickly. Knowledge-breadth evals (Opus 5.5 vs Opus 4.6 world map) and token-efficiency reporting are increasingly treated as better signals.

## Latest active insights

- Uniform 4-bit quantization can wreck a model. Dynamic, layer-aware quantization keeps heavily used layers at higher precision, and quantized-model recall has improved sharply as a result. ([What Runs Where? Local AI in Production — Ahmad Osman, Osmantic](../videos/2026-11-21-what-runs-where-local-ai-in-production-ahmad-osman-osmantic.md), 2026-11-21, relevance 2/5)
- LiteRT.js runs general ML models such as embedding models, while LiteRT-LM.js runs LLMs. Both delegate ops across WebGPU and Wasm execution backends and are imported as a plain JS library, needing fewer than 20 lines to load and run Gemma 4. ([Web AI at the Edge: Local Vector Search, Gemma 4, & hybrid cloud routing](../videos/2026-11-20-web-ai-at-the-edge-local-vector-search-gemma-4-hybrid-cloud.md), 2026-11-20, relevance 4/5)
- Gemma 4 comes in 2B, 4B, 12B, 26B MoE (4B active) and 31B sizes. Only the 2B and 4B support audio; 12B and above handle images and text only. ([Web AI at the Edge: Local Vector Search, Gemma 4, & hybrid cloud routing](../videos/2026-11-20-web-ai-at-the-edge-local-vector-search-gemma-4-hybrid-cloud.md), 2026-11-20, relevance 4/5)
- A land-vs-water coordinate eval, plotted as a world map, visualises model knowledge: Opus 4.6 gives a blurry map while Opus 5.5 is almost perfect. This suggests broad general knowledge matters more than frontier benchmark spikes. ([The Sweet Side of the Bitter Lesson — Thariq Shihipar, Independent](../videos/2026-11-19-the-sweet-side-of-the-bitter-lesson-thariq-shihipar-independ.md), 2026-11-19, relevance 4/5)
- Mythos, a model with expert-level ability to find software vulnerabilities and exploits, was released through Project Glasswing only to a limited set of companies, so cyberdefenders could find and patch vulnerabilities while broader safeguards were built. ([The making of Anthropic](../videos/2026-11-19-the-making-of-anthropic.md), 2026-11-19, relevance 2/5)
- The founders say Claude was far behind the frontier at public launch. Competitiveness came from compute plus stacked algorithmic 'compute multipliers', leading to Claude 3 Opus and then Sonnet 3.5, which made models useful for programming. ([The making of Anthropic](../videos/2026-11-19-the-making-of-anthropic.md), 2026-11-19, relevance 2/5)
- Early assistants were built toward 'helpful, harmless, honest'. Helpfulness and harmlessness came first, and honesty lagged badly: early models confidently endorsed an all-potato diet. ([The making of Anthropic](../videos/2026-11-19-the-making-of-anthropic.md), 2026-11-19, relevance 2/5)
- The founding bet was the 'big blob of compute' scaling trend: more compute and data yield predictably more capability and new emergent abilities, without hand-teaching planning or tool use. ([The making of Anthropic](../videos/2026-11-19-the-making-of-anthropic.md), 2026-11-19, relevance 2/5)
- For more hands-off work, Gemini 4 Argon offers two modes. Teamwork Preview uses many teams of subagents, while the /boost command works linearly and spins up subagents ad hoc. The speaker says Gemini 4 Argon is strong at long-horizon tasks. ([Build a "software factory" that doesn’t produce AI Slop](../videos/2026-11-19-build-a-software-factory-that-doesn-t-produce-ai-slop.md), 2026-11-19, relevance 3/5)
- Gemini 4 Hydrogen is the fast tier of the family, roughly tripling Terminal Bench 4 performance over Gemini 3.8 Flash while being about three times faster, and is available across Google surfaces. ([Post-Training Gemini 4 for Real Engineering Work — Melvin Johnson, Google DeepMind](../videos/2026-11-18-post-training-gemini-4-for-real-engineering-work-melvin-john.md), 2026-11-18, relevance 3/5)
- Gemini 4 Carbon is claimed to top several customer benchmarks while being substantially cheaper than the runner-up, making cost-per-task a headline differentiator alongside quality. ([Post-Training Gemini 4 for Real Engineering Work — Melvin Johnson, Google DeepMind](../videos/2026-11-18-post-training-gemini-4-for-real-engineering-work-melvin-john.md), 2026-11-18, relevance 3/5)
- Ultrafast is described as 8x faster at 6x the cost and works best for UI iteration with a hot-reloading dev server and light reasoning effort. It brings little benefit when build or test time dominates the task. ([New Ways of Working with Codex — Dominik Kundel & Charlie Guo, OpenAI](../videos/2026-11-17-new-ways-of-working-with-codex-dominik-kundel-charlie-guo-op.md), 2026-11-17, relevance 4/5)
- Benchmarks get trained against quickly. Gemini 3.1 Pro and later Gemini 4 Carbon aced his pelican-on-bicycle SVG test, including his held-out animal/vehicle variants, which weakened the benchmark. ([2026 in LLMs — Simon Willison](../videos/2026-11-17-2026-in-llms-simon-willison.md), 2026-11-17, relevance 3/5)
- He defines a "Fable class" model this way: if you can state a goal, provide the necessary tools, and the goal is possible, the model will solve it almost by brute force over runs of around 12 hours. Problem definition and tool provisioning become the key human skill. ([2026 in LLMs — Simon Willison](../videos/2026-11-17-2026-in-llms-simon-willison.md), 2026-11-17, relevance 3/5)
- Willison dates the coding-agent inflection point to November 2025. That is when the models became reliable enough to drive coding agents daily rather than as a novelty. ([2026 in LLMs — Simon Willison](../videos/2026-11-17-2026-in-llms-simon-willison.md), 2026-11-17, relevance 3/5)
- The 'bitterest lesson' is choosing the right training task: the RLHF paper showed a GPT-2-sized model with instruction-following training matched what would need roughly 'GPT-7 to 9' scale pretraining alone. (["Where is all the automation?" — Diogo Almeida, TypeSafe AI](../videos/2026-11-15-where-is-all-the-automation-diogo-almeida-typesafe-ai.md), 2026-11-15, relevance 3/5)
- GPT-6 Cyber rediscovered bugs that had been rated sec-moderate or sec-low and showed they were actually sec-high by bypassing architectural hardening the team had thought was bulletproof. This prompted re-prioritization and more defense-in-depth work. ([How Mozilla uses GPT-6 Cyber to find and fix Firefox vulnerabilities](../videos/2026-11-13-how-mozilla-uses-gpt-6-cyber-to-find-and-fix-firefox-vulnera.md), 2026-11-13, relevance 3/5)
- Model jaggedness can be exploited: assign each small agent the cheapest model that is good at its narrow task. Speaker claims DeepSeek V4 Flash is 137x cheaper than Fable per task, and Tencent's HY3 generated a strong 3D world for 60 cents despite being weak elsewhere. ([Why Domain-specific Agents Are the Future - Justin Schroeder, Standard Agents](../videos/2026-11-12-why-domain-specific-agents-are-the-future-justin-schroeder-s.md), 2026-11-12, relevance 4/5)
- Mythos and Fable use the same model weights but run with different classifier safeguards. Mythos access goes through a KYC-style Cyber Verification Program, and new thinking protections were added to prevent distillation. ([Building the Claude API for the frontier](../videos/2026-11-12-building-the-claude-api-for-the-frontier.md), 2026-11-12, relevance 3/5)
- The model string works as an implicit version. Deprecations are softened by keeping the old behaviour toggleable for a model or two and by offering beta headers for rollback, as was done when moving from extended thinking to adaptive thinking. ([Building the Claude API for the frontier](../videos/2026-11-12-building-the-claude-api-for-the-frontier.md), 2026-11-12, relevance 3/5)
- Google DeepMind found a bash-only harness scores about the same as richer tool sets but is roughly 2x cheaper per task, because stronger models chain more commands per call. The speaker argues token efficiency should be reported alongside accuracy, and that MCP code mode with structured outputs is a likely next step. ([AIE CODE: Google DeepMind, Anthropic, SpaceXSI, TypeSafe, Lovable, Ramp, etc (System One theme)](../videos/2026-11-12-aie-code-google-deepmind-anthropic-spacexsi-typesafe-lovable.md), 2026-11-12, relevance 4/5)

Lenient grading (general idea) and strict grading (exact details) diverge sharply: Opus understood about 70% of a skill's content at the general level but only about 22% of its specific details. The example is sorting incoming customer messages via the Claude API many times a day. ([Fable 5.2 or Opus? ## Open questions

- Will per-task cost and token efficiency become standard reported metrics alongside accuracy, and how should UIs expose cost to people delegating to agents?
- Does full-duplex voice (GPT-Live 1) become the default for agent interfaces, and what supervision and approval patterns does it require?
- How long will vendors keep deprecated behaviours (e.g. extended thinking) toggleable, and should developer portals document model strings as API versions?
- Will gated access (Mythos KYC, GPT-6 Cyber) spread to other capability tiers, fragmenting which models product teams can actually use?
- Is post-training task choice (RLHF 'bitterest lesson') now outpacing raw scaling as the source of capability gains?
