## Current best understanding

**AI is reshaping product team structure, and the gains are uneven (Nov 2026).** The clearest signal for how Parag's work gets organised: teams are shrinking to pods of 2–4 people with collapsed roles. Product roles move toward a **"definer"** (intent, specs, acceptance) and dev/QA roles toward a **"builder"** (8 Nov 2026). One short-term "cheat code" is to halve pod size and double the number of teams while keeping the same planning math. For a designer in agentic UX and design systems, this suggests design work will sit closer to the definer role. Specs, tokens and component contracts then become the main interface to builders and their agents.

**Productivity reality check.** A McKinsey survey of a few hundred large enterprises (reported 8 Nov 2026) found that only the top 20–25% see about 2x end-to-end improvement from AI in software development. Most still report modest 10–20% gains, though that is up from meager gains in a survey at the end of the prior year. The gap suggests that operating-model changes, such as the smaller pods and role shifts above, matter more than tool adoption alone.

## Open questions

- Does the "halve pods, double teams" acceleration hold beyond the short term, or does coordination cost (shared design systems, governance) erode it?
- Where do UX/design roles land in the definer/builder split? Is design absorbed into definer, or does it remain a distinct specialist role?
- What separates the top 20–25% of enterprises seeing ~2x gains from the majority stuck at 10–20%: tooling, process redesign or team structure?
- How should design system governance adapt when many small pods (and their agents) consume and modify components in parallel?
