## Current best understanding

**Distribution and surfaces for AI products (as of Nov 2026).** The most design-relevant shift is that agent platforms are becoming app stores with their own identity and ranking rules. OpenAI's plugin directory (4,000 plugins, Nov 2026) ranks discovery and in-conversation suggestions on post-install conversion, daily/weekly active use and retention. Submissions moved from a form to `plugin.json` metadata, with automated review on upload and auto-updates tied to MCP server deploys. Sign in with ChatGPT tripled Supabase's weekly installs and lets users bring their ChatGPT token entitlements into partner products. OpenAI Sites claims 8M hosted sites, and over 70% of OpenAI staff use it to replace decks and spreadsheets with interactive UIs. A related signal: non-technical studios used Claude Code to vibe-code narrow, per-role UIs on ArtCraft APIs, and that community is breaking cloned apps into reusable 2D/3D component and primitive libraries. The pattern is constrained, intent-specific apps assembled from shared primitives, which is directly relevant to design systems and AX. Ben's warning (Nov 2026) points the same way: agents make diverging implementations of formats trivial, so shared open specs matter more, not less.

**Go-to-market for agents.** Enterprises buy agents that automate one existing process end-to-end, for example HVAC fleet maintenance. The reasons are integration, trust (no all-powerful model) and repeatable efficacy, not a general replace-everything agent. Use cases cluster into five archetypes: software engineering, enterprise automation, research, personal assistants and robotics, the last two being hybrid edge-cloud. Security operations is the clearest proof point so far. Sophos reports 52% of MDR cases solved end-to-end, with response time cut from 38 min to 89 s, and Intuit reports 87% of closed findings were real vulnerabilities. Lovable's lesson still holds: its 2023 multi-agent orchestration made the product worse, and a single main LLM plus small retrieval models (late 2024) won until model capability caught up.

**How AI-native teams build (late 2026).** Reported gains vary widely. Ramp's shared Inspect agent ships ~75% of its code, and AI writes ~75% of Google's new code. Cognition saw 40x merged PRs against 2.7x headcount, and the Grok Bot team shipped 11,000 PRs in a month at ~0.5% revert. A large-org pipeline merged 84 of 242 PRs in two weeks. Yet McKinsey finds only the top 20–25% of enterprises at ~2x end-to-end, with most at 10–20%. Teams are shrinking to 2–4 person pods with 'definer' and 'builder' roles. Shared, company-wide agent baselines spread improvements without mandates. Google ships faster by silently routing experimental models to 100,000+ internal engineers.

## Open questions

- Will plugin-directory ranking on conversion and retention reward narrow, intent-specific agent apps over broad ones, and how should plugin UX be designed for in-conversation suggestion?
- Does Sign in with ChatGPT (token entitlements) become a de facto identity and billing layer that partner products and developer portals must design around?
- Can shared component and primitive libraries keep per-role vibe-coded apps coherent, or will agent-made forks fragment specs and design systems?
- Why do headline PR-throughput claims (10x–40x) diverge so sharply from McKinsey's typical 10–20% end-to-end gains, and which metrics actually measure quality?
- When does model capability make multi-agent orchestration reliable enough to beat single-LLM product architectures?
